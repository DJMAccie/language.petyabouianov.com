Nihongo Studio

I built this to learn enough Japanese for my trip.

There is a rule of thumb that about 20% of a language's words cover about 80% of what you actually hear. I had five months before mine, so that was the whole plan — and this is what came out of it.

Why this exists

I searched the internet for lists, and I had lessons with my sensei, who wrote down most of the language I was missing — I would point at something and ask what the word for it was. The lists here are built from what I actually needed.

I have been to Japan since, and the method held up: I had genuine conversations with people I had never met. I would like to offer that same preparation to you.

The lists are already made

Finding a decent vocabulary list is normally the first job. That part is done: 84 lists and 1,313 words, grouped by theme rather than alphabetised, so a list is something you can finish and use. City and Transport, Restaurant & Bar, Travel Essentials, Body Parts, Numbers, Basic Question Words, and eighty more.

The useful words come first in each list. Starter Kit holds 74 words and covers the greetings, pronouns and polite forms that most sentences lean on. Travel Essentials and Restaurant & Bar are the two to open before a trip.

These are the lists I used, pre-loaded so you do not have to go looking. Every list is editable once you have an account: delete what you already know, add words from your own reading, or split a large list into smaller ones. Your edits stay in your account and leave the shared library alone for everyone else.

Lessons come in fives

I took the shape of the daily lesson and the daily review from WaniKani, which I got on with. Anki and I never did — too much setting up before any learning. This is my attempt at the same habit with less ceremony and more words.

A lesson introduces five words. You see each word first and answer for it afterwards, so nothing is tested before it has been shown. Finishing a set brings up the next one, and the daily screen keeps track of how many sets you have cleared. Five is small on purpose. It takes a couple of minutes, and that is a far easier habit than an hour once a week.

The first five words of Starter Kit:

  • 初めましてHajimemashite Nice to meet you
  • いただけますかItadakemasu ka? Can I have a…? / Could I receive…?
  • いいねIi ne Nice! / That's good
  • 少しSukoshi A little
  • お会計お願いしますO kaikei onegaishimasu Can I have the bill please

Four answering modes cover the same words from different directions:

  • JP → EN shows the Japanese and asks for the meaning.
  • EN → JP gives the meaning and asks you to produce the Japanese.
  • Speech asks you to say the word aloud.
  • Multiple choice is the quick pass when you want speed over recall.

Kana and kanji are shown alongside romaji rather than instead of it, so you can read a word from the first day and pick up the script as you go. Words you get wrong come back sooner than words you know, and every word carries an accuracy figure, so you can see what is sticking. Typed answers accept romaji and Enter submits.

Review works on what you got wrong

The review queue holds words you have already met and not yet mastered. It puts the words that are due first, then the ones you have struggled with, so a short session goes where it is needed. Words leave the queue as you get them right consistently.

You can use it without an account

The whole app is open. No sign-up wall, no trial, no limits on which lists you can open. Without an account your progress is saved in your own browser, which is fine on a single device. Making an account keeps that progress, syncs it across devices, and lets you edit and create your own lists — sign in or make one whenever you want that. Signing in with Google or Apple creates a separate account from a username and password, so pick one route and stay with it.

Accounts store your lists and progress and nothing else.

It is free, and donations are genuinely appreciated

Nihongo Studio is free in full. There is no advertising and nothing costs money. One person builds and pays for it, which covers the hosting and the time to keep the word lists and quizzes in good shape.

A donation is welcome and never expected. If the app has been useful and you want to put something back, it goes straight into keeping it running.
